Build log · ESP32-C3 fan controller

From a 555 sketch to a panelised order

A four-channel PC fan controller, designed in KiCad over a handful of sessions and sent to PCBWay fully assembled. This is the route it actually took — including the wrong turns, which were the expensive part.

Stage 01

The scope moved before a single part was placed

The opening question was for the simplest possible circuit to hold a 4-pin PC fan at a fixed speed. The honest answer is a 555 timer generating 25 kHz PWM — or, simpler still, a 9 V supply instead of 12 V and no electronics at all.

That answer was correct and immediately obsolete: if the board was going to exist, it should drive four fans and take commands over WiFi. The 555 never made it into a schematic. What replaced it was a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 on a socket, four fan headers, and the small amount of glue needed to get 12 V in and PWM out.

Design rule

The ESP32-C3 strapping pins are not a suggestion. D9 in particular has a documented boot failure when a transistor is attached to it, so all eight fan signals were placed on D1–D7 and D10 — exactly eight clean pins, with nothing to spare.

Stage 02

The schematic is generated, not drawn

Every connection lives in one Python file as a (ref, pin) → net table. A single command rebuilds the whole sheet from it: placement, wires, labels, structural repair, and a netlist audit that diffs the result back against the table.

That indirection paid for itself the first time a channel needed re-assigning. Swapping FAN1 with FAN2 is a two-line edit and a re-run, not an afternoon of re-drawing.

The expensive mistake

Two full wiring attempts were scrapped before the cause surfaced. Symbol libraries store pin offsets with +y up; the schematic canvas uses +y down. Absolute pin position is therefore place_y - local_y, not plus.

Getting that sign wrong mirrors every pin about its own symbol origin, so wires attach to the pin on the opposite side of the part — the XIAO's VBUS resolved to D9/BOOT. ERC passes cleanly, because landing on the wrong real pin is still a perfectly valid connection. Only a pin-by-pin netlist audit catches it.

Two smaller traps sat behind it. KiCad rewrites the file with each field on its own line as soon as the GUI touches it, so any parser keyed to the single-line form silently finds zero symbols. And symbols defined with extends — most fixed-voltage regulators — inherit their pins from a base symbol, so copying the derived symbol alone yields a part with no pins at all.

Stage 03

Placement by hand, because the board is the constraint

Footprints were imported programmatically with their nets attached, then arranged by hand. The board is 25 mm tall and every millimetre is spoken for: the XIAO occupies the left third, the power chain runs along the top, and the four fan channels stack on the right, each with its own three resistors.

The outline was fitted last, 1 mm clear of all copper, which took the board from 80 × 60 mm down to 72.9 × 25.0 mm — a 54% reduction in area before the final trim.

Watch the connector

The jack was initially placed mid-board, then moved to the edge, then tucked inward when its flange sat 1.9 mm proud of the outline. The tuck stopped at 1.8 mm: beyond that, F1's pad and the +12V_IN trace collide with the +12V_PROT trunk running vertically through the same corridor.

A 3 mm move was requested and refused with the reason, rather than applied and left to fail DRC.

Stage 04

Routing by hand — and ground on the back with real wires

Automatic routing was never attempted. The board was routed manually in the KiCad GUI, where the ratsnest and live DRC make a mistake visible the moment it happens.

Method that worked

Route ground as ordinary wires on the back side, and pour the plane only at the very end. A plane laid early hides missing connections — every stray pad looks connected because copper happens to touch it. Wiring ground explicitly means the ratsnest stays honest until the layout is genuinely finished.

Power widths came from IPC-2152 for 1 oz copper at a 10 K rise. A single uniform width does not fit: the corridor around the XIAO is a 2.54 mm pad pitch. So every power segment starts at its target and steps down only where DRC objects.

Two GND segments stayed at 0.2 mm, wedged between XIAO pads. Sampling along their length found ground-plane copper beneath at 28 of 33 points, and the XIAO's ground pad is a through-hole tied into the plane — so the plane carries that return in parallel and width is not the limit there. A temporary marker layer flagged every undersized segment during rework, then was removed.

Stage 05

The plane goes last, with a hole cut for the antenna

Only once every net was routed did the ground pour go down on the back side: 1417 mm² of copper with thermal relief on pads. Unconnected items dropped from 16 to 1 the moment it filled.

Find the antenna properly

The XIAO is rotated −90° on this board, which puts pins 7 and 8 at the same y — the module's top edge, opposite the USB connector. That is where the PCB antenna sits. The keep-out spans that edge out to the board boundary and stops 0.52 mm short of those two pads; a first attempt ran 1.4 mm further and DRC correctly rejected it for swallowing them.

Stage 06

The fab package is where the real formats bite

One script produces gerbers, Excellon drill, BOM and pick-and-place. Three defaults in that chain are wrong for a real order.

Gerbers export every layer unless told otherwise

A bare kicad-cli pcb export gerbers wrote a 152 KB User_Eco1.gbr containing the internal review markers. The layer list has to be explicit.

Column headers are not negotiable at JLCPCB

KiCad writes Ref, Val, Package, PosX, PosY, Rot, Side. JLCPCB wants Designator, Mid X, Mid Y, Layer, Rotation — different names, different order, Top rather than top. The coordinates were right all along; only the header was rejected.

BOM and CPL are matched designator by designator

Collapsing four resistors to R1-R4 reads well to a human and fails the import: the CPL lists them individually, so the range matches nothing. Full comma lists only.

The part with no symbol

The XIAO is socketed, so what actually gets assembled is two 1×7 female headers — and they have no schematic symbol at all. Their holes are the 14 through-hole pads of the module's own footprint.

Invented designators (U1-SKT1) fail, because the fab matches BOM against CPL and those exist in neither. The working answer is to let U1 be the socket position: its comment describes the headers, and the module is plugged in afterward. That is the physical truth — sockets sit there, not a module.

Stage 07

Two fabs, two different packages

JLCPCB's catalogue is SMD-oriented and carried none of the through-hole parts, so that route means assembling 16 SMD positions and hand-soldering the rest. PCBWay offers through-hole assembly as a standard service, so there the whole board is populated — all 23 positions, BOM and CPL both including the THT parts.

Because PCBWay's staff read the BOM, the notes column does real work there: C1 is marked through-hole, not SMD, and U1 carries the full explanation of the socket arrangement. Both survived into the quote intact.

Still open on the quote

U1 is quoted at qty 1 — 10 headers for 10 boards. Two are needed per board. The quantity column tracks the designator list, so it cannot express “one footprint, two parts”; the count was moved into the value field (2x 1x7 female header) and the note, but the pricing followed the column.

Worth €0.80 and one email before assembly starts.

A cheaper route was investigated and rejected. Four boards panelised on one 100 × 100 mm panel is exactly 100.0 mm with zero gap, and the board cannot shrink: the +12V_PROT traces already sit at the 0.5 mm design-rule minimum from both edges. Three-up fits, but means hand-soldering roughly 240 0603 resistors for the saving.

Before the panels ship

Check the assembly preview for rotation. KiCad and the fabs use different zero orientations for the same packages, so U2 (SOT-223 — the tab must land on the wide pad) and D1 (cathode direction) need eyes on them. The resistors, ceramic cap and polyfuse are unpolarised and cannot be got wrong.
